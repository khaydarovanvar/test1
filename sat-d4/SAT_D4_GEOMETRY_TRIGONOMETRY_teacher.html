SAT Math 4.1 — Lines, Angles & Triangles

Explanations with worked examples · diagrams for every rule · Desmos questions | Teacher copy — with solutions · Grade 11 · Ellipse International School

1Angles on a line and at a point

straight line = 180° | full turn = 360° | vertical angles are equal

Complementary angles add to 90°; supplementary to 180°. Vertical (opposite) angles made by two crossing lines are always equal. Most angle-chasing questions are one of these three facts applied twice.

Example 1. Two angles are complementary. One is 3x° and the other (2x + 10)°. Find x.
Solution.

3x + 2x + 10 = 90, so 5x = 80.

x = 16.

Example 2. Two angles on a straight line are (4x)° and (x + 30)°. Find x.
Solution.

4x + x + 30 = 180, so 5x = 150.

x = 30.

Example 3. Two lines cross. One angle is 65°. Find the other three.
Solution.

The vertical angle is 65°; the two adjacent ones are each 180 − 65 = 115°.

2Parallel lines and a transversal

corresponding and alternate angles are equal | co-interior angles add to 180°

When a line crosses two parallels, every angle is either equal to a given one or supplementary to it. Find the equal pairs first; everything else follows from 180°.

Example 1. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal and one angle is 72°. What is its corresponding angle?
Solution.

Corresponding angles are equal.

72°.

Example 2. What is the co-interior angle to that 72°?
Solution.

Co-interior angles add to 180°.

180 − 72 = 108°.

Example 3. In the figure above, angles 4 and 5 are what kind of pair?
Solution.

They sit between the parallels on opposite sides of the transversal: alternate interior, so they are equal.

3Triangle angle facts

angles sum to 180° | an exterior angle equals the two remote interior angles

The exterior-angle shortcut saves a step: instead of finding the third angle and subtracting from 180, add the two far angles directly.

Example 1. A triangle has angles 50° and 60°. Find the third.
Solution.

180 − 50 − 60 = 70°.

Example 2. For that triangle, what is the exterior angle at the 60° vertex?
Solution.

It equals the two remote interior angles: 50 + 70 = 120°. Checking: 180 − 60 = 120.

Example 3. A triangle’s angles are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. Find the largest.
Solution.

The parts total 9, and 180/9 = 20° per part.

The largest is 4 × 20 = 80°.

4Isosceles and equilateral triangles

equal sides face equal angles | equilateral → every angle 60°

The tick marks on a diagram are doing real work: two marked sides mean two equal base angles. This turns most isosceles questions into a single equation.

Example 1. An isosceles triangle has apex angle 40°. Find each base angle.
Solution.

The two base angles are equal: (180 − 40)/2.

70° each.

Example 2. An isosceles triangle has a base angle of 50°. Find the apex angle.
Solution.

Both base angles are 50°.

180 − 100 = 80°.

Example 3. Why can an isosceles triangle not have two angles of 100°?
Solution.

They would total 200°, already above 180°. The equal angles in an isosceles triangle must each be below 90°.

5The triangle inequality

any two sides must add to more than the third

Given two sides a and b, the third side x satisfies |a − b| < x < a + b. Questions ask for the possible values, or for the largest or smallest whole number that works.

Example 1. Two sides are 7 and 10. What values can the third side take?
Solution.

10 − 7 < x < 10 + 7.

3 < x < 17.

Example 2. For those sides, what is the largest possible whole-number third side?
Solution.

x must be below 17.

16.

Example 3. Can a triangle have sides 4, 5 and 9?
Solution.

4 + 5 = 9, which is not more than 9.

No — it collapses flat.

6Polygons and quadrilaterals

interior angles of an n-gon sum to (n − 2) × 180° | exterior angles always sum to 360°

The exterior-angle fact is the quicker route for regular polygons: each exterior angle is 360/n, and the interior angle is its supplement. In a parallelogram opposite angles are equal and adjacent ones add to 180°.

Example 1. Find the sum of the interior angles of a hexagon.
Solution.

(6 − 2) × 180 = 720°.

Example 2. Find each interior angle of a regular hexagon.
Solution.

Each exterior angle is 360/6 = 60°.

Interior = 180 − 60 = 120°.

Example 3. A regular polygon has interior angles of 150°. How many sides?
Solution.

Each exterior angle is 30°, and they total 360°.

360/30 = 12 sides.

SAT Math 4.2 — Similarity & Congruence

Explanations with worked examples · diagrams for every rule · Desmos questions | Teacher copy — with solutions · Grade 11 · Ellipse International School

1When are two triangles similar?

AA — two pairs of equal angles is enough | similar means same shape, different size

You never need all three angles: if two match, the third must. Similar triangles have equal angles and proportional sides, and the SAT almost always gives you the angles and asks for a side.

Example 1. Two triangles have angles 40°, 60° and 40°, 80°. Are they similar?
Solution.

The first has third angle 80°, the second 60°. Both are 40, 60, 80.

Yes, by AA.

Example 2. Two right triangles each have an angle of 35°. Are they similar?
Solution.

Both have 90° and 35°, so the third is 55° in each.

Yes, by AA.

Example 3. Two triangles have sides 3, 4, 5 and 6, 8, 11. Are they similar?
Solution.

6/3 = 2 and 8/4 = 2, but 11/5 = 2.2.

No — the ratios must all match.

2The scale factor

scale factor k = new ÷ old | every matching length multiplies by k

Find k from the one pair of sides you are given, then apply it to the side you want. Pair the sides correctly: the side opposite the 30° angle in one triangle matches the side opposite the 30° angle in the other.

Example 1. A triangle with sides 3, 4, 5 is enlarged so the shortest side becomes 9. Find the scale factor.
Solution.

k = 9/3 = 3.

Example 2. For that enlargement, what is the new longest side?
Solution.

5 × 3 = 15.

Example 3. Triangle ABC has AB = 6; similar triangle DEF has DE = 15. If BC = 8, find EF.
Solution.

k = 15/6 = 2.5.

EF = 8 × 2.5 = 20.

3Area and volume scale differently

lengths × k | areas × k² | volumes × k³

This is the trap the SAT returns to. Doubling every length multiplies the area by 4 and the volume by 8. Going the other way, if areas are in the ratio 9 : 4, the lengths are in the ratio 3 : 2 — take the square root.

Example 1. Two similar rectangles have sides in the ratio 2 : 5. What is the ratio of their areas?
Solution.

Square the factor.

4 : 25.

Example 2. Two similar solids have volumes 8 cm³ and 216 cm³. Find the ratio of their heights.
Solution.

216/8 = 27, and the cube root of 27 is 3.

1 : 3.

Example 3. A model is built to scale 1 : 30. The model holds 2 litres. How much does the real tank hold?
Solution.

Volume scales by 30³ = 27,000.

2 × 27,000 = 54,000 litres.

4Similar triangles hidden in one figure

a line parallel to one side cuts off a similar triangle

When a figure contains a line parallel to a side, two similar triangles are sitting inside it. Redraw them separately, the same way round, before writing any ratio.

Example 1. In triangle ABC, DE is parallel to BC with AD = 4, DB = 6 and AE = 6. Find EC.
Solution.

AD/DB = AE/EC, so 4/6 = 6/EC.

EC = 9.

Example 2. Why are the two triangles similar?
Solution.

DE parallel to BC makes the corresponding angles equal, so AA applies.

Example 3. In that figure, AB = 10 and AC = 15. What is the scale factor from ADE to ABC?
Solution.

AD/AB = 4/10.

k = 2.5 going from the small triangle to the large one.

5Shadows, mirrors and indirect measurement

same sun angle → similar triangles → height/shadow is the same ratio for both

The classic SAT word problem. Write the ratio the same way round for both objects: height ÷ shadow for one equals height ÷ shadow for the other.

Example 1. A 6-foot pole casts a 4-foot shadow. A signpost casts an 18-foot shadow. Find its height.
Solution.

6/4 = h/18, so h = 18 × 1.5 = 27 feet.

Example 2. A 1.8 m person casts a 1.2 m shadow. A tree’s shadow is 8 m. Find the tree’s height.
Solution.

1.8/1.2 = 1.5, and 8 × 1.5 = 12 m.

Example 3. Why must the two shadows be measured at the same time?
Solution.

The sun’s angle changes through the day. Only at the same moment are the two triangles similar.

6Congruence

congruent = same shape and same size, so the scale factor is 1

Congruent triangles match exactly: SSS, SAS, ASA or AAS. Note that AAA gives similarity only, never congruence, because the sizes can differ.

Example 1. Two triangles have all three pairs of sides equal. Are they congruent?
Solution.

Yes, by SSS.

Example 2. Two triangles have all three pairs of angles equal. Are they congruent?
Solution.

Not necessarily — they are similar, but one could be an enlargement of the other.

Example 3. Triangles ABC and DEF are congruent with AB = 7. What is DE?
Solution.

Congruent means the scale factor is 1.

DE = 7.

SAT Math 4.3 — Right Triangles & Trigonometry

Explanations with worked examples · diagrams for every rule · Desmos questions | Teacher copy — with solutions · Grade 11 · Ellipse International School

1Pythagoras

a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse — the side opposite the right angle

Identify the hypotenuse before substituting. It is always the longest side and always opposite the right angle. Putting a leg where c belongs is the error that costs this topic its marks.

Example 1. A right triangle has legs 9 and 12. Find the hypotenuse.
Solution.

81 + 144 = 225.

c = √225 = 15.

Example 2. A right triangle has hypotenuse 13 and one leg 5. Find the other leg.
Solution.

Here 13 is c, so 25 + b² = 169.

b = √144 = 12.

Example 3. A drone flies 9 km east then 12 km north. How far is it from the start?
Solution.

The path makes a right angle.

√(81 + 144) = 15 km.

2The triples worth memorising

3-4-5 | 5-12-13 | 8-15-17 | 7-24-25 — and all their multiples

Recognising a triple turns a calculation into a glance. 9-12-15 is 3-4-5 tripled; 10-24-26 is 5-12-13 doubled. If two sides fit a triple, the third is immediate.

Example 1. A right triangle has legs 9 and 12. Which triple is this?
Solution.

3-4-5 multiplied by 3.

The hypotenuse is 5 × 3 = 15.

Example 2. A right triangle has hypotenuse 26 and one leg 10. Find the other leg.
Solution.

5-12-13 doubled.

The other leg is 12 × 2 = 24.

Example 3. Is a triangle with sides 8, 15, 17 right-angled?
Solution.

64 + 225 = 289 = 17².

Yes — the converse of Pythagoras.

3Special right triangles

45-45-90 → sides 1 : 1 : √2 | 30-60-90 → sides 1 : √3 : 2

These two appear constantly and are on the reference sheet. In a 30-60-90 the shortest side faces the 30° angle and is half the hypotenuse; the √3 side faces the 60°.

Example 1. A 45-45-90 triangle has legs of 5. Find the hypotenuse.
Solution.

5 × √2 = 5√2 ≈ 7.07.

Example 2. A 30-60-90 triangle has hypotenuse 10. Find the shortest side.
Solution.

The side opposite 30° is half the hypotenuse.

5.

Example 3. For that triangle, find the remaining side.
Solution.

It faces the 60° angle: 5√3 ≈ 8.66.

4SOH-CAH-TOA

sin = opp/hyp | cos = adj/hyp | tan = opp/adj

Label the three sides relative to the angle you are working with before choosing a ratio. Opposite and adjacent swap when you switch angles; the hypotenuse never moves.

Example 1. In a right triangle, sin A = 0.6 and the hypotenuse is 20. Find the side opposite A.
Solution.

sin A = opp/hyp, so opp = 0.6 × 20 = 12.

Example 2. A ramp makes an angle whose sine is 0.6 and is 20 feet long. How high is the top?
Solution.

The ramp is the hypotenuse.

0.6 × 20 = 12 feet.

Example 3. In a right triangle, the side adjacent to angle B is 8 and the hypotenuse is 17. Find cos B.
Solution.

cos B = adj/hyp = 8/17 ≈ 0.471.

5Finding an angle

use inverse trig: if tan θ = 3/4 then θ = tan⁻¹(3/4)

When the sides are known and the angle is not, the inverse functions give it. In Desmos type arctan, arcsin or arccos and make sure the calculator is in degrees.

Example 1. A right triangle has opposite 3 and adjacent 4. Find the angle, to the nearest degree.
Solution.

tan θ = 0.75, so θ = tan⁻¹(0.75) ≈ 37°.

Example 2. A ladder 10 m long reaches 8 m up a wall. What angle does it make with the ground?
Solution.

sin θ = 8/10 = 0.8.

θ = sin⁻¹(0.8) ≈ 53°.

Example 3. Why do those two answers add to about 90°?
Solution.

They are the two acute angles of similar right triangles — 3-4-5 in both cases.

6sin θ = cos(90° − θ)

the sine of an angle equals the cosine of its complement

In a right triangle the two acute angles add to 90°, and what is opposite one is adjacent to the other. So sin and cos swap. Questions set sin(expression) = cos(expression) and ask you to make the two angles add to 90.

Example 1. If sin 35° = cos x° and x is acute, find x.
Solution.

x = 90 − 35 = 55.

Example 2. In a right triangle the acute angles are (2x + 10)° and (3x − 20)°. Find x.
Solution.

They are complementary: 2x + 10 + 3x − 20 = 90, so 5x = 100.

x = 20.

Example 3. If sin A = 3/5 for an acute angle A, what is cos(90° − A)?
Solution.

3/5 — it is the same quantity, by the identity.

The ramp is the hypotenuse, and sin = opp/hyp.

0.6 × 20 = 12 feet. A common wrong answer divides 20 by 0.6 × 2 or similar — the clue is that the opposite side must be shorter than the hypotenuse.

P3Hard
In right triangle ABC, the right angle is at C and the hypotenuse AB = 13. If BC = 5, what is the length of AC?
12

AB is the hypotenuse, so 5² + AC² = 13².

AC = √144 = 12. This is the 5-12-13 triple.

P4Hard
A 30-60-90 triangle has a hypotenuse of length 10. What is the length of the side opposite the 30° angle?
5

In a 30-60-90 triangle the side opposite 30° is half the hypotenuse.

10/2 = 5. A common wrong answer is the side opposite 60°.

P5Hard
If sin 35° = cos x° and 0 < x < 90, what is the value of x?
55

sin θ = cos(90° − θ).

x = 90 − 35 = 55.

P6Hard
A 45-45-90 triangle has legs of length 5. What is the length of the hypotenuse?
5√2

In a 45-45-90 triangle the sides are in the ratio 1 : 1 : √2.

The hypotenuse is 5√2 ≈ 7.07. A common wrong answer doubles the leg instead.

P7Very
hard
In a right-triangle design on a banner, the two acute angles measure (2x + 10)° and (3x − 20)°, so sin((2x + 10)°) = cos((3x − 20)°). What is the value of x?

The identity holds exactly when the two angles are complementary.

2x + 10 + 3x − 20 = 90 gives 5x = 100, so x = 20. The angles are 50° and 40°.

P8Very
hard

53°

P9Very
hard

A common wrong answer comes from subtracting instead of adding the squares.

P10Very
hard

3/5

SAT Math 4.4 — Circles

Explanations with worked examples · diagrams for every rule · Desmos questions | Teacher copy — with solutions · Grade 11 · Ellipse International School

1Circumference and area

C = 2πr | A = πr² — both are on the reference sheet

Almost every circle question starts by finding r. If you are given the area, take the square root; if the circumference, divide by 2π. Then answer what was asked.

Example 1. A circle has area 64π. Find its circumference.
Solution.

πr² = 64π gives r = 8.

C = 2π(8) = 16π.

Example 2. A circle has circumference 18π. Find its area.
Solution.

2πr = 18π gives r = 9.

A = π(81) = 81π.

Example 3. A circular path encloses 100π square metres. How long is the path?
Solution.

r = 10, so the path is the circumference.

2π(10) = 20π metres.

2Arcs and sectors

arc = (θ/360) × 2πr | sector area = (θ/360) × πr²

A sector is a fraction of the whole circle, and the fraction is the angle over 360. Work out the whole first, then take the slice.

Example 1. A circle of radius 9 has a sector with central angle 80°. Find its area.
Solution.

Whole area = 81π, and 80/360 = 2/9.

(2/9)(81π) = 18π.

Example 2. For that same circle, find the arc length of the sector.
Solution.

Whole circumference = 18π.

(2/9)(18π) = 4π.

Example 3. A sector of a circle of radius 6 has area 6π. Find its central angle.
Solution.

Whole area 36π, and 6π/36π = 1/6.

(1/6)(360) = 60°.

3Central and inscribed angles

a central angle equals its arc | an inscribed angle is half its arc

The vertex position decides the rule: at the centre, the angle equals the arc; on the circle, it is half. The special case worth knowing cold is that an angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle.

Example 1. A central angle cuts an arc of 70°. What is the central angle?
Solution.

70° — they are equal.

Example 2. An inscribed angle cuts that same 70° arc. What is the inscribed angle?
Solution.

Half the arc.

35°.

Example 3. Triangle ABC is inscribed in a circle with AB a diameter. What is angle ACB?
Solution.

The arc AB is 180°, and the inscribed angle is half.

90°.

4Tangents and chords

a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact

A tangent gives you a right angle for free, which usually means Pythagoras is next. A radius drawn perpendicular to a chord also bisects it.

Example 1. A tangent touches a circle of radius 5 at P. From an external point Q, PQ = 12. Find OQ.
Solution.

Angle OPQ is 90°, so OQ² = 25 + 144.

OQ = 13.

Example 2. Why is triangle OPQ right-angled?
Solution.

OP is a radius to the point of contact, and a tangent is perpendicular to the radius there.

Example 3. A chord of length 16 sits in a circle of radius 10. How far is it from the centre?
Solution.

The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, giving legs 8 and d with hypotenuse 10.

d = √(100 − 64) = 6.

5The equation of a circle

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r², centre (h, k), radius r

Read the centre with the signs flipped and remember the right-hand side is r squared, not r. This formula is not on the reference sheet.

Example 1. Find the centre and radius of (x − 3)² + (y + 1)² = 9.
Solution.

Centre (3, −1); radius 3, because 9 = 3².

Example 2. Write the equation of a circle with centre (−2, 5) and radius 4.
Solution.

(x + 2)² + (y − 5)² = 16.

Example 3. Does the point (6, −1) lie on the circle in the first example?
Solution.

(6 − 3)² + (−1 + 1)² = 9 + 0 = 9.

Yes, it is exactly on the circle.

6Completing the square for a circle

group the x terms and the y terms, then halve and square each coefficient

An equation given as x² + y² + Dx + Ey = F has to be rearranged before you can read anything off. Complete the square twice, once for x and once for y.

Example 1. Find the radius of x² + y² − 8x + 6y = 11.
Solution.

(x − 4)² − 16 + (y + 3)² − 9 = 11.

(x − 4)² + (y + 3)² = 36, so r = 6.

Example 2. Find its centre.
Solution.

(4, −3) — the signs flip.

Example 3. Find the radius of x² + y² + 4x − 2y = 20.
Solution.

(x + 2)² + (y − 1)² = 20 + 4 + 1 = 25.

r = 5.

SAT Math 4.5 — Area & Volume

Explanations with worked examples · diagrams for every rule · Desmos questions | Teacher copy — with solutions · Grade 11 · Ellipse International School

1Areas of plane figures

triangle ½bh | parallelogram bh | trapezoid ½(a + b)h | circle πr²

The height is always the perpendicular distance, not the slanted side. A triangle drawn leaning over still uses the vertical height.

Example 1. A triangle has base 12 and perpendicular height 7. Find its area.
Solution.

½(12)(7) = 42.

Example 2. A trapezoid has parallel sides 8 and 14 and height 5. Find its area.
Solution.

½(8 + 14)(5) = ½(22)(5) = 55.

Example 3. A parallelogram has base 9 and slant side 6, with perpendicular height 5. Find its area.
Solution.

Use the perpendicular height, not the slant.

9 × 5 = 45.

2Composite figures

split the shape into pieces you know, or subtract a hole from a whole

Decide whether adding or subtracting is quicker, then label every piece before calculating. A shaded region is nearly always one area minus another.

Example 1. A square of side 10 has a circle of radius 5 cut from it. Find the shaded area left over.
Solution.

100 − 25π ≈ 100 − 78.54 = 21.46.

Example 2. A rectangle 12 by 8 has a triangle of base 12 and height 8 removed. What fraction remains?
Solution.

Rectangle 96, triangle 48.

Half remains.

Example 3. An L-shape is a 10 by 6 rectangle with a 4 by 3 rectangle removed from one corner. Find its area.
Solution.

60 − 12 = 48.

3Volume of prisms and cylinders

V = (area of the base) × height | cylinder V = πr²h

Any solid with the same cross-section all the way up follows one rule: find the base area, multiply by the height. The reference sheet gives these, so the work is choosing the base.

Example 1. A cylinder has radius 3 and height 8. Find its volume.
Solution.

π(9)(8) = 72π ≈ 226.19.

Example 2. A rectangular tank measures 40 by 25 by 30 centimetres. Find its volume.
Solution.

40 × 25 × 30 = 30,000 cm³, which is 30 litres.

Example 3. A triangular prism has a base triangle of area 15 and length 10. Find its volume.
Solution.

15 × 10 = 150.

4Cones, pyramids and spheres

cone ⅓πr²h | pyramid ⅓Bh | sphere ⁴⁄₃πr³

The one-third is the whole difficulty: a cone is a third of the cylinder that would contain it. All three formulas are on the reference sheet, so check you have copied the fraction.

Example 1. A cone has radius 6 and height 10. Find its volume.
Solution.

(1/3)π(36)(10) = 120π.

Example 2. A sphere has radius 3. Find its volume.
Solution.

(4/3)π(27) = 36π.

Example 3. A cylinder and a cone share the same radius and height. How do their volumes compare?
Solution.

The cone is exactly one third of the cylinder.

5Scaling a solid

lengths × k | surface area × k² | volume × k³

Changing one dimension multiplies the volume by that factor alone. Changing every dimension by k multiplies the volume by k cubed. Read carefully which the question means.

Example 1. A cylinder’s radius is doubled and its height is unchanged. What happens to the volume?
Solution.

V depends on r².

The volume is multiplied by 4.

Example 2. A cube’s edges are all tripled. What happens to its volume?
Solution.

Every dimension triples.

Volume × 3³ = 27.

Example 3. A cylinder’s height alone is doubled. What happens to the volume?
Solution.

Only one dimension changes.

Volume doubles.

6Density and unit conversion

density = mass ÷ volume | for volume the conversion factor is cubed

Density questions are a rate in disguise. For units, remember 1 litre = 1000 cm³ while 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ — the two are not the same factor.

Example 1. A block has mass 540 g and volume 60 cm³. Find its density.
Solution.

540/60 = 9 g/cm³.

Example 2. A tank holds 30,000 cm³. How many litres is that?
Solution.

30,000 ÷ 1000 = 30 litres.

Example 3. How many cubic centimetres are in 1 cubic metre?
Solution.

100³ = 1,000,000. The length factor 100 gets cubed.

SAT Math 4.6 — Coordinate Geometry

Explanations with worked examples · diagrams for every rule · Desmos questions | Teacher copy — with solutions · Grade 11 · Ellipse International School

1Distance between two points

d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²] — it is Pythagoras in disguise

Draw the horizontal and vertical legs and the formula becomes obvious. Because both differences are squared, the order of subtraction does not matter.

Example 1. Find the distance between (1, 2) and (5, 5).
Solution.

Legs 4 and 3.

√(16 + 9) = 5.

Example 2. Find the distance between (−2, 1) and (4, 9).
Solution.

Legs 6 and 8.

√(36 + 64) = 10.

Example 3. Why does subtracting the other way round give the same answer?
Solution.

Squaring removes the sign: (−4)² = 4².

2Midpoint

midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2) — average each coordinate

Average the x’s and average the y’s, separately. If you are given one endpoint and the midpoint, work backwards: the midpoint is as far past itself again.

Example 1. Find the midpoint of (1, 2) and (5, 5).
Solution.

((1+5)/2, (2+5)/2) = (3, 3.5).

Example 2. The midpoint of AB is (4, 1) and A is (2, −3). Find B.
Solution.

B is the same step again: (4 + 2, 1 + 4) = (6, 5).

Example 3. Find the midpoint of (−3, 7) and (5, −1).
Solution.

((−3+5)/2, (7−1)/2) = (1, 3).

3Slope

m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) — rise over run

Keep the two points in the same order on top and bottom. A positive slope rises left to right; a horizontal line has slope 0 and a vertical line has no slope at all.

Example 1. Find the slope through (1, 2) and (5, 5).
Solution.

(5 − 2)/(5 − 1) = 3/4.

Example 2. Find the slope through (2, 7) and (6, −1).
Solution.

(−1 − 7)/(6 − 2) = −8/4 = −2.

Example 3. What is the slope of the line through (3, 4) and (3, 9)?
Solution.

The run is 0, so the slope is undefined — the line is vertical.

4Parallel and perpendicular

parallel → equal slopes | perpendicular → slopes multiply to −1 (negative reciprocals)

To get a perpendicular slope, flip the fraction and change the sign. The perpendicular to a slope of 2/3 is −3/2.

Example 1. A line has slope 2/3. What is the slope of a line perpendicular to it?
Solution.

Flip and negate.

−3/2.

Example 2. Is y = 3x + 1 parallel to y = 3x − 7?
Solution.

Both have slope 3.

Yes — parallel, and never meeting.

Example 3. A line through (0, 4) is perpendicular to y = −½x. Write its equation.
Solution.

The perpendicular slope is 2.

y = 2x + 4.

5The equation of a circle in the plane

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²

This is coordinate geometry’s one memorised formula, and it is not on the reference sheet. The centre’s coordinates appear with their signs flipped.

Example 1. A circle has centre (2, −3) and radius 5. Write its equation.
Solution.

(x − 2)² + (y + 3)² = 25.

Example 2. Find the centre and radius of (x + 4)² + (y − 1)² = 49.
Solution.

Centre (−4, 1), radius 7.

Example 3. Is (5, −3) on the circle with centre (2, −3) and radius 5?
Solution.

(5 − 2)² + 0 = 9, not 25.

No — it is inside.

6Area on a grid

for a right-angled or axis-aligned figure, read the legs straight off the grid

When a figure sits on the coordinate axes, you rarely need a formula beyond the basics: count the horizontal and vertical lengths and apply the ordinary area rule.

Example 1. Find the area of a triangle with vertices (0, 0), (6, 0) and (0, 4).
Solution.

Legs 6 and 4 along the axes.

½(6)(4) = 12.

Example 2. Find the area of a rectangle with vertices (1, 1), (7, 1), (7, 5) and (1, 5).
Solution.

Width 6, height 4.

24.

Example 3. Find the area of a triangle with vertices (2, 1), (8, 1) and (5, 6).
Solution.

Base 6 along y = 1, height 5.

½(6)(5) = 15.

Part 2 — Test-Style Questions (12 questions · hard and very hard)

Twelve questions, hardest last. Write your answer in the box — there are no options to choose from, so show the working that gets you there. Sketch the points on a quick set of axes first. Nearly every question in this topic is Pythagoras, an average, or a slope — and the sketch tells you which.

#LevelQuestionAnswerSolution
P1Hard
What is the distance between the points (−2, 1) and (4, 9) in the xy-plane?
10

The horizontal difference is 6 and the vertical difference is 8.

√(36 + 64) = √100 = 10.

P2Hard
What is the midpoint of the segment joining (−3, 7) and (5, −1)?
(1, 3)

Average each coordinate: ((−3 + 5)/2, (7 − 1)/2).

(1, 3). A common wrong answer subtracts the coordinates instead of averaging them.

P3Hard
What is the slope of the line passing through (2, 7) and (6, −1)?
−2

(−1 − 7)/(6 − 2) = −8/4 = −2.

A common wrong answer inverts the fraction, giving run over rise.

P4Hard
A line has a slope of 2/3. What is the slope of any line perpendicular to it?
−3/2

Perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals: flip the fraction and change the sign.

−3/2. A common wrong answer flips without negating; another negates without flipping.

P5Hard
A circle has centre (2, −3) and radius 5. Write the equation of that circle.
(x − 2)² + (y + 3)² = 25

Signs flip inside the brackets and the right side is r².

(x − 2)² + (y + 3)² = 25. A common wrong answer uses r instead of r².

P6Hard
A triangle has vertices (2, 1), (8, 1) and (5, 6). What is its area, in square units?
15

The base lies along y = 1 from x = 2 to x = 8, so it is 6 long, and the height is 6 − 1 = 5.

½(6)(5) = 15.

P7Very
hard
The midpoint of segment AB is (4, 1). If A has coordinates (2, −3), what are the coordinates of B?
(6, 5)

The midpoint is the average, so B is the same step again: (4 + 2, 1 + 4).

(6, 5). A common wrong answer is the midpoint of A and the given midpoint.

P8Very
hard
Line k passes through (0, 4) and is perpendicular to the line y = −½x + 3. What is the equation of line k?
y = 2x + 4

The perpendicular slope to −½ is 2, and the y-intercept is given as 4.

y = 2x + 4. A common wrong answer flips the fraction without changing the sign.

P9Very
hard
In the xy-plane, a circle has equation (x + 4)² + (y − 1)² = 49. What is the sum of the coordinates of its centre and its radius?
4

Centre (−4, 1) and radius 7.

−4 + 1 + 7 = 4.

P10Very
hard
A square has two opposite vertices at (1, 1) and (7, 5). What is the length of its diagonal?
2√13

The diagonal joins the two given points: √(36 + 16) = √52.

√52 = 2√13 ≈ 7.21, so 2√13. A common wrong answer is the same value unsimplified — the SAT expects the simplified radical.

P11Very
hard
Line m has equation y = 3x − 2. Which line is parallel to m and passes through (1, 5)?
y = 3x + 2

Parallel means the same slope, 3, and 5 = 3(1) + b gives b = 2.

y = 3x + 2. A common wrong answer is the original line; another does not pass through (1, 5).

P12Very
hard
Points A(0, 0), B(6, 0) and C(6, 8) form a triangle. What is its perimeter?
24

AB = 6 and BC = 8, and the right angle at B makes AC = 10 by the 6-8-10 triple.

6 + 8 + 10 = 24. A common wrong answer omits the hypotenuse; another is twice the area.

The one habit to build. Distance is Pythagoras; midpoint is an average. Confusing the two is the commonest error here, and a thirty-second sketch prevents it.
