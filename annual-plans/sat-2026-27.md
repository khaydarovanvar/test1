# The SAT in 2026–2027 — what it is now, and when it runs

Written 6 October 2026. The SAT has been **fully digital and adaptive** since 2024: taken in
College Board's **Bluebook** app on a laptop or tablet, scored 400–1600, no essay, no paper option
outside accommodations. Nothing about the format changes for 2026–27 — the changes this year are to
the practice materials and the app, not the test.

## The test

| | Reading and Writing | Math |
|---|---|---|
| Modules | 2 × 27 questions | 2 × 22 questions |
| Time | 2 × 32 min | 2 × 35 min |
| Total | 54 questions, 64 min | 44 questions, 70 min |

98 questions, **2 h 14 min** plus a 10-minute break. Reading and Writing comes first.

**Adaptive by module.** Everyone sits the same first module, mixing easy, medium and hard
questions. Performance on it decides whether the second module is the harder, higher-scoring one or
the easier one. A module cannot be re-entered once left, though questions may be flagged and
revisited inside it.

**Tools in Bluebook:** a built-in **Desmos graphing calculator available for the whole maths
section**, a reference sheet of formulas, annotation, a countdown timer, and question flagging.
Students may also bring their own approved calculator.

## Maths content

44 questions across four domains:

| Domain | Share | Questions | Content |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Algebra | ≈35% | 13–15 | linear equations in one and two variables, systems of linear equations, linear functions, linear inequalities |
| Advanced Math | ≈35% | 13–15 | quadratic, polynomial, exponential, radical, rational and absolute-value equations and functions; equivalent forms; nonlinear systems |
| Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | ≈15% | 5–7 | ratio, rate, proportion, percentage, units; one- and two-variable data, scatterplots and lines of best fit, two-way tables, probability, sample statistics, margin of error, evaluating study design |
| Geometry and Trigonometry | ≈15% | 5–7 | lines and angles, triangles and similarity, the Pythagorean theorem and special right triangles, right-triangle trigonometry, circles — arcs, sectors, radians and the equation of a circle; area and volume |

About 75% are multiple choice with four options; the rest are student-produced response, typed in.
There is **no calculus, no proof writing, no matrix or vector work, no complex numbers** — the
ceiling is roughly our Grade 10 algebra.

### Against our own plans

For a pupil who has finished our Grade 9 course, SAT Maths is almost entirely revision: linear and
quadratic work, functions, exponentials and rational expressions are Grade 8–10 algebra, and the
geometry is Grade 8–9 plus the circle work of Grade 9. Two parts of the test have thin counterparts
in the national programme and need deliberate teaching:

* **Problem-Solving and Data Analysis.** Two-way tables, scatterplots and the line of best fit,
  margin of error, sample statistics and judging a study's design appear nowhere in grades 8–10;
  the national programme reaches statistics only in Grade 11, and then without inference. This is
  5–7 questions — about 100 points of scaled score.
* **Speed on multi-step word problems.** 44 questions in 70 minutes is 95 seconds a question. Our
  practice sets are harder per item but untimed.

Radians and arc length are a Cambridge insert in Grade 10, not national content, so a pupil on the
national stream alone may meet them first on the SAT.

## Topics and subtopics in full

College Board's own taxonomy: each section has four **domains**, each domain a fixed set of
**skill and knowledge testing points**. The third level below — what the questions actually ask —
is written out for teaching.

### Maths — 44 questions

#### Algebra · ≈35% · 13–15 questions · 5 skills

1. **Linear equations in one variable** — solving; equations with no solution or infinitely many;
   choosing a coefficient that makes an equation have no solution; building an equation from a
   worded situation.
2. **Linear equations in two variables** — writing and solving; slope-intercept, standard and
   point-slope form; intercepts; graphing; interpreting the slope and the intercept in context.
3. **Linear functions** — function notation; constant rate of change; functions from tables and
   from descriptions; interpreting f(x) in context.
4. **Systems of two linear equations in two variables** — substitution, elimination, graphing;
   one solution, none, or infinitely many; parallel and identical lines; building a system from a
   worded situation.
5. **Linear inequalities in one or two variables** — solving; graphing a half-plane; systems of
   inequalities and feasible regions; constraint problems.

#### Advanced Math · ≈35% · 13–15 questions · 3 skills

1. **Equivalent expressions** — expanding and factoring; operations on polynomials; rational
   expressions; radicals and rational exponents; completing the square; rearranging a formula for
   one variable; rewriting an expression to reveal a feature.
2. **Nonlinear equations in one variable and systems of equations in two variables** — quadratics
   by factoring, square roots, completing the square and the formula; the discriminant and the
   number of real solutions; radical equations and extraneous solutions; rational equations;
   absolute-value equations; exponential equations; higher-degree polynomial equations;
   linear–quadratic systems.
3. **Nonlinear functions** — quadratic functions in standard, vertex and factored form and what
   each reveals (vertex, axis of symmetry, zeros, intercepts, maximum or minimum); exponential
   growth and decay, growth factors and percentage change; polynomial functions — zeros,
   multiplicity, end behaviour; rational functions and asymptotes; transformations and
   translations of graphs; evaluating and composing functions from graphs and tables.

#### Problem-Solving and Data Analysis · ≈15% · 5–7 questions · 7 skills

1. **Ratios, rates, proportional relationships and units** — unit rates; scale drawings; unit
   conversion, including compound units such as density; proportions in similar figures.
2. **Percentages** — percentage of a quantity; percentage increase and decrease; successive
   changes; working backwards to the original amount.
3. **One-variable data: distributions and measures of centre and spread** — mean, median, mode,
   range; standard deviation **compared, never computed**; the effect of an outlier or of adding
   or removing a value; reading dot plots, histograms, box plots and frequency tables.
4. **Two-variable data: models and scatterplots** — line or curve of best fit; interpreting the
   slope and intercept of a fitted model; predicting from a model; residuals; choosing between a
   linear and an exponential model; reading bar graphs, line graphs and two-way tables.
5. **Probability and conditional probability** — probability from a table; relative-frequency and
   two-way tables; conditional probability.
6. **Inference from sample statistics and margin of error** — what a margin of error and a
   confidence interval mean; how sample size changes the margin of error; which conclusions a
   sample supports.
7. **Evaluating statistical claims: observational studies and experiments** — random sampling
   versus random assignment; when causation may be claimed; to what population a result
   generalises.

#### Geometry and Trigonometry · ≈15% · 5–7 questions · 4 skills

1. **Area and volume** — perimeter and area of polygons and circles; surface area and volume of
   prisms, cylinders, cones, pyramids and spheres (formulas are given on the reference sheet);
   composite solids; area and volume scale factors.
2. **Lines, angles and triangles** — vertical, complementary and supplementary angles; parallel
   lines cut by a transversal; the angle sum and the exterior angle of a triangle; interior angles
   of a polygon; congruence and similarity; the triangle inequality.
3. **Right triangles and trigonometry** — the Pythagorean theorem and Pythagorean triples; the
   30–60–90 and 45–45–90 triangles; sine, cosine and tangent in a right triangle; the
   complementary relation sin x = cos(90° − x); similar right triangles.
4. **Circles** — circumference and area; arc length and sector area; central and inscribed angles;
   chords and tangents; radians and conversion to degrees; the equation of a circle in the
   xy-plane, and completing the square to find its centre and radius.

**Question formats.** About three quarters are multiple choice with four options; the rest are
student-produced response, typed in. In those, an answer may be at most 5 characters (6 if
negative); a fraction or a decimal is accepted; percent signs, commas and mixed numbers are not.

**Not on the test:** calculus, limits, proof writing, matrices, vectors, complex numbers,
logarithms as a topic in their own right, and any trigonometry beyond the right triangle and the
radian.

### Reading and Writing — 54 questions

Short passages of 25–150 words, one question each, grouped by domain and running easiest to
hardest within each group.

| Domain | Share | Questions | Skills |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Information and Ideas | ≈26% | 12–14 | central ideas and details · command of evidence, textual · command of evidence, quantitative (graphs and tables) · inferences |
| Craft and Structure | ≈28% | 13–15 | words in context · text structure and purpose · cross-text connections |
| Standard English Conventions | ≈26% | 11–15 | boundaries — sentence boundaries and punctuation: commas, semicolons, colons, dashes, apostrophes · form, structure and sense — verb tense and agreement, subject–verb agreement, pronoun agreement and clarity, modifier placement, parallel structure, possessives and plurals |
| Expression of Ideas | ≈20% | 8–12 | rhetorical synthesis — using given notes to meet a stated goal · transitions |

Deadlines close 23:59 US Eastern time — that is **09:59 next morning in Tashkent**, so treat the
deadline as the day after on our clock.

The September administration is a US school-day sitting and is not offered internationally. The
other seven dates are offered outside the US, but **which centres actually open seats in Uzbekistan
varies date by date**, so a date is only real once a centre shows free seats in the pupil's College
Board account.

## Registering from Uzbekistan

1. A College Board account at **my.collegeboard.org**, then *Register for the SAT*.
2. Pick the date, then search test centres by country. Seats in Tashkent go early — register as
   soon as a sitting opens rather than near the deadline.
3. **Use the passport** as the admission ID; it is the document accepted most consistently at
   centres outside the US, and the name must match the registration exactly.
4. Fee: **$68** in the US, about **$117** from outside it; a few countries carry a further regional
   surcharge. Fee waivers are a US-only benefit.
5. Scores arrive in roughly two weeks, sometimes up to four.
6. The sitting is digital: the pupil brings a charged laptop or tablet with Bluebook installed and
   the exam pre-downloaded, or uses a centre machine where one is provided.
