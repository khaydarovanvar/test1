# The SAT in 2026–2027 — what it is now, and when it runs

Written 6 October 2026. The SAT has been **fully digital and adaptive** since 2024: taken in
College Board's **Bluebook** app on a laptop or tablet, scored 400–1600, no essay, no paper option
outside accommodations. Nothing about the format changes for 2026–27 — the changes this year are to
the practice materials and the app, not the test.

## The test

| | Reading and Writing | Math |
|---|---|---|
| Modules | 2 × 27 questions | 2 × 22 questions |
| Time | 2 × 32 min | 2 × 35 min |
| Total | 54 questions, 64 min | 44 questions, 70 min |

98 questions, **2 h 14 min** plus a 10-minute break. Reading and Writing comes first.

**Adaptive by module.** Everyone sits the same first module, mixing easy, medium and hard
questions. Performance on it decides whether the second module is the harder, higher-scoring one or
the easier one. A module cannot be re-entered once left, though questions may be flagged and
revisited inside it.

**Tools in Bluebook:** a built-in **Desmos graphing calculator available for the whole maths
section**, a reference sheet of formulas, annotation, a countdown timer, and question flagging.
Students may also bring their own approved calculator.

## Maths content

44 questions across four domains:

| Domain | Share | Questions | Content |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Algebra | ≈35% | 13–15 | linear equations in one and two variables, systems of linear equations, linear functions, linear inequalities |
| Advanced Math | ≈35% | 13–15 | quadratic, polynomial, exponential, radical, rational and absolute-value equations and functions; equivalent forms; nonlinear systems |
| Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | ≈15% | 5–7 | ratio, rate, proportion, percentage, units; one- and two-variable data, scatterplots and lines of best fit, two-way tables, probability, sample statistics, margin of error, evaluating study design |
| Geometry and Trigonometry | ≈15% | 5–7 | lines and angles, triangles and similarity, the Pythagorean theorem and special right triangles, right-triangle trigonometry, circles — arcs, sectors, radians and the equation of a circle; area and volume |

About 75% are multiple choice with four options; the rest are student-produced response, typed in.
There is **no calculus, no proof writing, no matrix or vector work, no complex numbers** — the
ceiling is roughly our Grade 10 algebra.

### Against our own plans

For a pupil who has finished our Grade 9 course, SAT Maths is almost entirely revision: linear and
quadratic work, functions, exponentials and rational expressions are Grade 8–10 algebra, and the
geometry is Grade 8–9 plus the circle work of Grade 9. Two parts of the test have thin counterparts
in the national programme and need deliberate teaching:

* **Problem-Solving and Data Analysis.** Two-way tables, scatterplots and the line of best fit,
  margin of error, sample statistics and judging a study's design appear nowhere in grades 8–10;
  the national programme reaches statistics only in Grade 11, and then without inference. This is
  5–7 questions — about 100 points of scaled score.
* **Speed on multi-step word problems.** 44 questions in 70 minutes is 95 seconds a question. Our
  practice sets are harder per item but untimed.

Radians and arc length are a Cambridge insert in Grade 10, not national content, so a pupil on the
national stream alone may meet them first on the SAT.

Deadlines close 23:59 US Eastern time — that is **09:59 next morning in Tashkent**, so treat the
deadline as the day after on our clock.

The September administration is a US school-day sitting and is not offered internationally. The
other seven dates are offered outside the US, but **which centres actually open seats in Uzbekistan
varies date by date**, so a date is only real once a centre shows free seats in the pupil's College
Board account.

## Registering from Uzbekistan

1. A College Board account at **my.collegeboard.org**, then *Register for the SAT*.
2. Pick the date, then search test centres by country. Seats in Tashkent go early — register as
   soon as a sitting opens rather than near the deadline.
3. **Use the passport** as the admission ID; it is the document accepted most consistently at
   centres outside the US, and the name must match the registration exactly.
4. Fee: **$68** in the US, about **$117** from outside it; a few countries carry a further regional
   surcharge. Fee waivers are a US-only benefit.
5. Scores arrive in roughly two weeks, sometimes up to four.
6. The sitting is digital: the pupil brings a charged laptop or tablet with Bluebook installed and
   the exam pre-downloaded, or uses a centre machine where one is provided.

## Preparation — the free official material

| Resource | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Bluebook** full-length practice tests | the only practice that reproduces the adaptive second module, so the only practice whose predicted score means anything. The set is rotated; the earliest tests have been retired as unrepresentative, so use the newest the app offers |
| **Official Digital SAT Prep on Khan Academy** | College Board's own free course: diagnostic, then targeted practice by skill |
| **Digital SAT Question Bank** | official questions filterable by domain, skill and difficulty — the right tool for building a lesson on one weakness |
| **Paper practice tests (PDF)** | linear, non-adaptive versions at `satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/practice-tests/paper`, with scoring guides and answer explanations. Intended for paper accommodations, but fine as printed classwork |
