# AP Mathematics — the four courses, unit by unit

A reference for 2026–2027. The College Board offers **four** mathematics Advanced Placement
courses; there is no AP Algebra and no AP Geometry.

| Course | Units | Typical US load | Prerequisite | Where it would sit here |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **AP Precalculus** | 4 (three assessed) | one year, ≈ 140–160 h | Geometry + Algebra 2 | Grade 10 |
| **AP Calculus AB** | 8 | one year, ≈ 140–160 h | Precalculus incl. trigonometry | Grade 11 |
| **AP Calculus BC** | 10 (AB's 8 + 2) | one year, same clock, faster | Precalculus incl. trigonometry | Grade 11 (strong group) |
| **AP Statistics** | 5 *(revised, Fall 2026)* | one year, ≈ 140–160 h | one year of algebra | Grade 10 or 11, as a separate subject |

BC contains AB entire and reports an **AB subscore**, so a school runs one or the other, not both,
for the same student.

## What changed for this school year

Students taught in 2026–27 sit the **May 2027** exams, and all three changes below land on them:

* **AP Statistics is a different course.** The revised Course and Exam Description took effect
  **Fall 2026**: the old nine units are consolidated into **five**, the second-year-algebra
  prerequisite is gone, and several topics were removed — analysing departures from linearity,
  combining random variables, the geometric distribution, the chi-square goodness-of-fit test, and
  the whole of the old Unit 9 (inference for slopes). The May 2027 exam is **fully digital** in
  Bluebook, with no paper free-response booklet and no Investigative Task.
* **AP Calculus AB and BC: content unchanged, exam re-timed.** The Fall 2026 CED carries
  clarifications only. From May 2027 the multiple-choice section becomes **42 questions in 100
  minutes** (was 45 in 105).
* **AP Precalculus: multiple choice goes from 40 to 42 questions** from May 2027. Free response
  is unchanged.

## AP Precalculus

Four units. **Unit 4 is not assessed on the exam** — it exists for states and schools whose own
standards require it. Exam weights below are for the multiple-choice section.

### Unit 1 — Polynomial and Rational Functions · 30–40%

1.1 Change in tandem · 1.2 Rates of change · 1.3 Rates of change in linear and quadratic functions ·
1.4 Polynomial functions and rates of change · 1.5 Polynomial functions and complex zeros ·
1.6 Polynomial functions and end behaviour · 1.7 Rational functions and end behaviour ·
1.8 Rational functions and zeros · 1.9 Rational functions and vertical asymptotes ·
1.10 Rational functions and holes · 1.11 Equivalent representations of polynomial and rational
expressions · 1.12 Transformations of functions · 1.13 Function model selection and assumption
articulation · 1.14 Function model construction and application

### Unit 2 — Exponential and Logarithmic Functions · 27–40%

2.1 Change in arithmetic and geometric sequences · 2.2 Change in linear and exponential functions ·
2.3 Exponential functions · 2.4 Exponential function manipulation · 2.5 Exponential function context
and data modelling · 2.6 Competing function model validation · 2.7 Composition of functions ·
2.8 Inverse functions · 2.9 Logarithmic expressions · 2.10 Inverses of exponential functions ·
2.11 Logarithmic functions · 2.12 Logarithmic function manipulation · 2.13 Exponential and
logarithmic equations and inequalities · 2.14 Logarithmic function context and data modelling ·
2.15 Semi-log plots

### Unit 3 — Trigonometric and Polar Functions · 30–35%

3.1 Periodic phenomena · 3.2 Sine, cosine and tangent · 3.3 Sine and cosine function values ·
3.4 Sine and cosine function graphs · 3.5 Sinusoidal functions · 3.6 Sinusoidal function
transformations · 3.7 Sinusoidal function context and data modelling · 3.8 The tangent function ·
3.9 Inverse trigonometric functions · 3.10 Trigonometric equations and inequalities · 3.11 The
secant, cosecant and cotangent functions · 3.12 Equivalent representations of trigonometric
functions · 3.13 Trigonometry and polar coordinates · 3.14 Polar function graphs · 3.15 Rates of
change in polar functions

### Unit 4 — Functions Involving Parameters, Vectors and Matrices · not assessed

4.1 Parametric functions · 4.2 Parametric functions modelling planar motion · 4.3 Parametric
functions and rates of change · 4.4 Parametrically defined circles and lines · 4.5 Implicitly
defined functions · 4.6 Conic sections · 4.7 Parametrisation of implicitly defined functions ·
4.8 Vectors · 4.9 Vector-valued functions · 4.10 Matrices · 4.11 The inverse and determinant of a
matrix · 4.12 Linear transformations and matrices · 4.13 Matrices as functions · 4.14 Matrices
modelling contexts

**Mathematical practices** — procedural and symbolic fluency (39–48% of the exam), multiple
representations, and communication and reasoning.

**Exam (May 2026 form; 42 MCQ from May 2027)**

| Section | Questions | Time | Share | Calculator |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| I A | 28 MCQ | 80 min | 43.75% | not permitted |
| I B | 12 MCQ | 40 min | 18.75% | graphing, required |
| II A | 2 FRQ — function concepts; modelling a non-periodic context | 30 min | 18.75% | graphing, required |
| II B | 2 FRQ — modelling a periodic context; symbolic manipulations | 30 min | 18.75% | not permitted |

## AP Calculus AB and BC

Ten units. **AB is units 1–8**; **BC is all ten**, plus the BC-only topics marked below inside the
shared units. Weights are for the multiple-choice section.

| Unit | AB | BC |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 Limits and continuity | 10–12% | 4–7% |
| 2 Differentiation: definition and fundamental properties | 10–12% | 4–7% |
| 3 Differentiation: composite, implicit and inverse functions | 9–13% | 4–7% |
| 4 Contextual applications of differentiation | 10–15% | 6–9% |
| 5 Analytical applications of differentiation | 15–18% | 8–11% |
| 6 Integration and accumulation of change | 17–20% | 17–20% |
| 7 Differential equations | 6–12% | 6–9% |
| 8 Applications of integration | 10–15% | 6–9% |
| 9 Parametric equations, polar coordinates, vector-valued functions | — | 11–12% |
| 10 Infinite sequences and series | — | 17–18% |

**Unit 1 — Limits and continuity.** Can change occur at an instant; defining limits and limit
notation; estimating limits from graphs and from tables; limits by algebraic properties; limits by
algebraic manipulation; selecting a procedure; the squeeze theorem; connecting representations of
limits; types of discontinuity; continuity at a point; continuity over an interval; removing
discontinuities; infinite limits and vertical asymptotes; limits at infinity and horizontal
asymptotes; the intermediate value theorem.

**Unit 2 — Differentiation: definition and fundamental properties.** Average and instantaneous rate
of change; the derivative and derivative notation; estimating derivatives at a point;
differentiability and continuity; the power rule; constant, sum, difference and constant-multiple
rules; derivatives of sin x, cos x, eˣ and ln x; the product rule; the quotient rule; derivatives of
tan, cot, sec and csc.

**Unit 3 — Composite, implicit and inverse functions.** The chain rule; implicit differentiation;
differentiating inverse functions; differentiating inverse trigonometric functions; selecting
procedures for derivatives; higher-order derivatives.

**Unit 4 — Contextual applications of differentiation.** Meaning of the derivative in context;
straight-line motion — position, velocity, acceleration; rates of change in non-motion contexts;
related rates; local linearity and linearisation; L'Hôpital's rule.

**Unit 5 — Analytical applications of differentiation.** The mean value theorem; the extreme value
theorem, global and local extrema, critical points; increasing and decreasing intervals; the first
derivative test; the candidates test for absolute extrema; concavity; the second derivative test;
sketching a function and its derivatives; connecting f, f′ and f″; optimisation — setting up and
solving; behaviour of implicit relations.

**Unit 6 — Integration and accumulation of change.** Accumulations of change; approximating area
with Riemann sums; summation and definite-integral notation; the fundamental theorem of calculus and
accumulation functions; behaviour of accumulation functions; properties of definite integrals; the
FTC and definite integrals; antiderivatives and indefinite integrals; integration by substitution;
long division and completing the square; **BC:** integration by parts, linear partial fractions,
improper integrals; selecting an antidifferentiation technique.

**Unit 7 — Differential equations.** Modelling with differential equations; verifying solutions;
sketching and reasoning with slope fields; **BC:** Euler's method; separation of variables — general
and particular solutions; exponential models; **BC:** logistic models.

**Unit 8 — Applications of integration.** Average value of a function; position, velocity and
acceleration by integration; accumulation in applied contexts; area between curves as functions of
x and of y, and with more than two intersections; volumes by cross sections — squares, rectangles,
triangles, semicircles; volumes by discs and by washers, about the axes and about other axes;
**BC:** arc length and distance travelled.

**Unit 9 — BC only: parametric, polar and vector-valued.** Differentiating parametric equations;
second derivatives and concavity; arc length of a parametric curve; differentiating and integrating
vector-valued functions; motion problems; polar coordinates and differentiation in polar form; area
of a polar region; area between two polar curves.

**Unit 10 — BC only: infinite sequences and series.** Convergence and divergence; geometric series;
the nth-term test; the integral test; harmonic series and p-series; comparison tests; the
alternating series test; the ratio test; absolute and conditional convergence; the alternating
series error bound; Taylor polynomials; the Lagrange error bound; radius and interval of convergence
of a power series; Taylor and Maclaurin series; functions as power series.

**Exam (May 2026 form)** — Section I: 45 MCQ in 105 min (Part A 30 questions, 60 min, no
calculator; Part B 15 questions, 45 min, graphing calculator), 50%. Section II: 6 FRQ in 90 min
(Part A 2 questions, 30 min, calculator; Part B 4 questions, 60 min, no calculator), 50%.
**From May 2027:** 42 MCQ in 100 min — Part A 29 questions in 62 min without a calculator, Part B
13 questions in 38 min with one. Free response is unchanged.

## AP Statistics — revised, effective Fall 2026

Five units, built around the statistical problem-solving cycle. The four practices come from the
American Statistical Association's GAISE framework: **formulate questions, collect data, analyse
data, interpret results.**

| Unit | Exam weight |
|---|---:|
| 1 Exploring one-variable data and collecting data | 20–30% |
| 2 Probability, random variables and probability distributions | 15–25% |
| 3 Inference for categorical data: proportions | 15–25% |
| 4 Inference for quantitative data: means | 10–20% |
| 5 Regression analysis | 10–20% |

**Unit 1** — statistical questions; displays and description of one-variable data; shape, centre,
spread, outliers; summary statistics and the normal model; census, sample and experiment; sampling
methods and bias; experimental design and random assignment; scope of inference.

**Unit 2** — randomness and simulation; probability rules; conditional probability and independence;
discrete random variables, their mean and standard deviation; the binomial distribution; the normal
distribution; sampling distributions and the central limit theorem.

**Unit 3** — the sampling distribution of a sample proportion; confidence intervals and significance
tests for one proportion and for a difference of two proportions; chi-square tests for independence
and homogeneity.

**Unit 4** — the t distribution; confidence intervals and significance tests for one mean, for
paired data, and for a difference of two means.

**Unit 5** — scatterplots and association; the correlation coefficient r; the least-squares
regression line; residuals and residual plots; r²; interpreting slope and intercept in context.

The old framework's sampling-distribution unit is now spread across units 2, 3 and 4, and data
collection — formerly its own unit — opens the course inside unit 1.

**Exam.** May 2026 was hybrid digital: 40 MCQ in 90 min (50%), then 5 free-response questions plus
an Investigative Task in 90 min (37.5% + 12.5%), graphing calculator and formula sheet throughout.
**May 2027 is fully digital in Bluebook:** 42 multiple-choice questions with four options each
instead of five, and four free-response questions, 90 minutes a section, each section half the
score. The Investigative Task is gone.

> The unit titles, weights and exam shape above are confirmed across several independent summaries
> of the Fall 2026 CED; the College Board's own site was unreachable from the machine this was
> written on, so the topic-level lists for the five new units are reconstructed from the published
> consolidation and should be checked line by line against the official CED PDF before a syllabus
> goes to the AP Course Audit.

## How much of this the school already teaches

Our grades 9–11 run the Uzbekistan national programme with a Cambridge IGCSE / AS–A Level layer.
Against the AP frameworks the overlap is uneven: **AP Precalculus is nearly covered already, AP
Calculus AB is about two-thirds covered, BC and Statistics are not.**

### AP Precalculus against Grade 10 Algebra (102 h) and Grade 10 Geometry (68 h)

| AP unit | Already taught | Not taught |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Polynomial and rational | the function, domain and range, operations on functions, composite and inverse functions, properties of a function, transformations of graphs, rational equations and inequalities, linear and quadratic modelling; quadratics from Grade 9 | complex zeros and multiplicity, end-behaviour notation, holes, equivalent algebraic forms, average rate of change as a theme, model selection from data |
| 2 Exponential and logarithmic | the exponential function, exponential equations and inequalities, the logarithm, the logarithmic function, transforming logarithmic expressions, logarithmic equations and inequalities, applications; progressions in the Quarter IV revision | competing model validation, residuals, **semi-log plots** |
| 3 Trigonometric and polar | trigonometric functions and their graphs, periodic processes, arcsin/arccos/arctan/arccot and their graphs, trigonometric equations and inequalities, radians and arc length (Cambridge insert) | sinusoidal transformations and data modelling, graphs of sec, csc and cot, **polar coordinates, polar graphs, rates of change in polar functions** |
| 4 Parameters, vectors, matrices *(not assessed)* | vectors in the plane and transformations (Grade 10 geometry), vectors and the scalar product in space (Grade 11 geometry) | parametric functions, conic sections, matrices and linear transformations |

Estimated new teaching time: **12–18 hours**, chiefly polar functions, sinusoidal and exponential
data modelling, and semi-log plots. Everything else is re-framing what Grade 10 already does.

### AP Calculus AB against Grade 11 Algebra and Calculus (102 h)

| AP unit | Already taught | Not taught |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Limits and continuity | the concept of a limit, the ratio of increments | limit notation and properties, limits from graphs and tables, limits by manipulation, the squeeze theorem, types of discontinuity, continuity on an interval, infinite limits and asymptotes, the IVT |
| 2 Definition and properties | the derivative and its geometric and physical meaning, rules of differentiation | differentiability versus continuity, numerical estimation of derivatives, derivatives of tan, cot, sec, csc |
| 3 Composite, implicit, inverse | the derivative of a composite function | **implicit differentiation, derivatives of inverse and inverse trigonometric functions,** higher-order derivatives |
| 4 Contextual applications | physical meaning, approximate calculations, modelling with the derivative | signed velocity and acceleration, **related rates, L'Hôpital's rule** |
| 5 Analytical applications | investigating a function and sketching its graph, extremum problems in geometry, physics and economics | **the mean value and extreme value theorems,** the candidates test, concavity and the second derivative test, reading f, f′ and f″ together |
| 6 Integration and accumulation | the antiderivative, the table of integrals, rules of integration, the definite integral, Newton–Leibniz, the trapezium rule, by parts and partial fractions (inserts) | **Riemann sums and summation notation, accumulation functions and FTC part 1,** properties of definite integrals, **integration by substitution** |
| 7 Differential equations | forming and solving differential equations (insert) | **slope fields,** particular solutions from initial conditions, exponential models |
| 8 Applications of integration | applications of the definite integral | average value of a function, motion by integration, **area between two curves,** **volumes by cross sections, discs and washers** |

Estimated new teaching time: **30–40 hours** on a 102-hour course — so AB needs either four hours a
week in Grade 11 or a deliberate trade against the national combinatorics, statistics and
probability block (27 h) and complex numbers (5 h), none of which the AP Calculus exam assesses.

### AP Calculus BC

Everything in the AB gap, plus units 9 and 10 and the BC extras — improper integrals, Euler's
method, logistic models, arc length, parametric, vector and polar calculus, and the whole of
infinite sequences and series, which alone is 17–18% of the exam. **Nothing in grades 9–11 touches
series.** BC is roughly **70–85 hours** of new content: realistic only as a 5-hour-a-week Grade 11
course, or spread over grades 10 and 11 with AP Precalculus compressed.

### AP Statistics

| AP unit | Already taught | Not taught |
|---|---|---|
| 1 One-variable data and collecting data | statistical data and its types, mean, median, mode, deviation and standard deviation (Grade 11, 7 h) | shape, outliers and boxplots as a vocabulary, the normal model as a description, **sampling methods and bias, experimental design, random assignment, scope of inference** |
| 2 Probability and distributions | random events, operations on events and Euler–Venn diagrams, addition and multiplication of probabilities, the binomial and normal distributions, combinatorics (Grade 11, 17 h; Grade 10, 7 h) | simulation, conditional probability and independence formally, mean and standard deviation of a random variable, **sampling distributions and the CLT** |
| 3 Inference for proportions | — | **all of it** |
| 4 Inference for means | — | **all of it** |
| 5 Regression analysis | studying the relationship between two sets of data (Grade 11, 3 h) | scatterplots and r properly, the least-squares line, residual plots, r², interpretation in context |

About **30 of the 100 hours** have a counterpart in the current grades 10–11 plans — descriptive
statistics, elementary probability, the binomial and normal distributions — so roughly **70%** is
new. Statistical inference, half the exam, has no counterpart anywhere in the national programme.
It cannot be fitted into the existing algebra courses; it needs its own course of about 100 hours.

### A workable route

| Grade | Course | Added hours needed |
|---|---|---:|
| 10 | AP Precalculus on the existing Grade 10 Algebra plan | 12–18 |
| 11 | AP Calculus AB on the existing Grade 11 plan | 30–40 |
| 11 | AP Calculus BC instead, for a selected group | 70–85 |
| 10 or 11 | AP Statistics as a separate subject | ≈ 100 |

Two administrative points before any of this is advertised to parents: a course may be called "AP"
and its students entered for the exam only after the school passes the **AP Course Audit** with a
syllabus for that course, and the exams must be sat at an authorised test centre in May.
