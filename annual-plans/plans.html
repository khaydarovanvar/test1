Two streams, one standard in mathematics

Three annual plans built from the Uzbekistan Grade 8 calendar-thematic plan and Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics Stage 9. The special class keeps the national programme and absorbs Cambridge; the Cambridge class keeps Stage 9 and absorbs the national depth. Both finish Grade 8 at the same level.

Why the two plans are mixed in opposite directions

Cambridge Stage 9 is lighter than the Uzbekistan Grade 8 programme. Stage 9 has no quadratic equations, no trigonometry, no circle theorems, no vectors, no coordinate geometry and no algebraic fractions. A 6-hour Cambridge course taught on its own would finish the year behind the 5-hour special class.

Special class · national backbone

The national plan is kept lesson-for-lesson — same topics, same order, same quarter totals — so it stays compliant with the Ministry programme and the state control works.

Its 31 flexible lessons (“Practical and interdisciplinary problems”, “Chapter revision problems”, and the Quarter IV revision blocks) are filled with the Cambridge Stage 9 topics the national programme never touches. Stage 9 is covered in full without adding a single hour.

Cambridge class · Stage 9 backbone

All 15 Stage 9 units and all 6 Stage 9 projects are taught in sequence.

The national depth topics are built in as full teaching blocks: quadratic equations (18 h), right-triangle trigonometry (10 h), circle theorems (7 h), vectors and the coordinate method (9 h), algebraic fractions (11 h), roots and rational exponents (8 h), inequalities and modulus (6 h).

Same calendar. The Cambridge quarter totals (54 / 42 / 60 / 48) are the standard Uzbek quarter lengths — 9, 7, 10 and 8 weeks — multiplied by 6 hours. Both streams run on the same quarters, the same holidays and the same reporting dates.

Public holidays and the reserve-lesson protocol

Holidays that fall on a working school day remove real lessons. The plans keep the official hour totals unchanged and absorb the loss through lessons marked Reserve — so a lost lesson never costs new content.

Expected loss. Four to six public holidays a year fall on a working school day. At 3 hours a week that costs 2–3 lessons; at 2 hours a week, 1–2; on the 6-hour Cambridge course, 4–6.

Absorption protocol

  1. At the start of each quarter, count the red days falling on the days this class has mathematics.
  2. Remove exactly that many reserve lessons from the same quarter, working backwards from the end of the quarter.
  3. Never remove an assessment, and never remove a lesson that introduces new content.
  4. If a quarter has fewer reserve lessons than lessons lost, merge two consecutive lessons carrying the same topic title — the national plan repeats titles across 2–3 lessons for exactly this purpose.
  5. Record every adjustment in the register so the quarter still shows the official hour total.

School holidays

Autumn ≈ 1–7 November · Winter ≈ 30 December – 10 January · Spring ≈ 20–30 March. These are already excluded from the quarter hour totals and need no adjustment.

Confirm annually. Weekdays are calculated for 2026–2027. The two movable Islamic holidays and any transferred working days must be checked against the Cabinet of Ministers resolution published each year, then this protocol re-applied.
