From Slope to Derivative

Grade 11 · Lesson notes

These five words look like five topics. They are one story, and they all answer the same question: how steep is the graph here? Read the chain below, then follow it one step at a time.

01 Slope of a straight line toʻgʻri chiziqning burchak koeffitsiyenti · наклон прямой

Take any two points on a straight line. Go across, then go up. Divide.

slope = riserun = y2 − y1x2 − x1 = 63 = 2

Slope 2 means: every 1 step to the right, the line climbs 2 steps up.

A straight line is equally steep everywhere. Pick any two points on it and you get the same answer — this is exactly what makes a line straight.

02 Gradient — same number, harder question egri chiziqning qiyaligi · крутизна кривой

Gradient is another word for slope. In your textbook it is the burchak koeffitsiyenti / угловой коэффициент.

Now look at a curve. It is gentle near the bottom and steep further along. So this question has no answer: “What is the gradient of y = x2?”

Only this one does: “What is the gradient of the curve at this point?” Everything that follows exists to answer it.

03 Secant line — steepness between two points kesuvchi · секущая

We only know how to find the slope of a straight line. So draw one: take two points on the curve, P and Q, and join them. That line is the secant.

Call the step across h. Then P is (x, f(x)) and Q is (x+h, f(x+h)), and the slope is:

f(x+h) − f(x)h

For f(x) = x2 with P(1, 1) and Q(3, 9): (9 − 1) / (3 − 1) = 4.

What does 4 mean? It is the average steepness from x = 1 to x = 3 — like average speed on a journey: total distance over total time. It says nothing about one single moment.

04 Tangent line — steepness at one point urinma · касательная

Now we want the steepness exactly at P. One point is not enough: the rise is 0 and the run is 0, and 0/0 is meaningless.

So keep two points — but slide Q down the curve towards P, and watch the secant slopes. For f(x) = x2 at x = 1:

h10.50.10.010.001→ 0
secant slope32.52.12.012.0012

The numbers do not jump around. They walk steadily towards 2. And the secant lines themselves settle onto one line: the tangent at P. It touches the curve at P and points in the same direction the curve is going there.

Careful“Touches at one point” describes the direction near P. A tangent is still allowed to cross the curve somewhere far away — that does not stop it being a tangent at P.

05 Derivative — the limit gets a name hosila · производная

“The number the secant slopes walk towards” is a mouthful. Mathematics gives it a name and a symbol — the derivative:

f′(x) = limh→0 f(x+h) − f(x)h

Do it once by hand for f(x) = x2, so the table above stops being magic:

(x+h)2 − x2h = x2 + 2xh + h2 − x2h = 2xh + h2h = 2x + h
We may cancel h because h ≠ 0 — Q has not reached P yet, it is only close.
Now let h → 0:   f′(x) = 2x,   so f′(1) = 2.
The same 2 the table was walking towards — found exactly, not guessed.
The whole chain in one sentence
The derivative is the gradient of the tangent — and the tangent is what the secant becomes when the two points meet.

Check yourself · Oʻzingizni tekshiring · Проверьте себя

  1. A straight line passes through (2, 3) and (6, 11). Find its slope. Then find it again using two different points on the same line — what do you notice?
  2. For f(x) = x2, find the slope of the secant between x = 2 and x = 2.1.
  3. Using f′(x) = 2x, write down the gradient of the tangent at x = 2. Compare it with your answer to question 2. Why are they close but not equal?

Answers1 — slope 2, and any two points give 2, because the line is equally steep everywhere.  2 — (4.41 − 4) / 0.1 = 4.1.  3 — the tangent gradient is 4. The secant used a step of h = 0.1, so it gives the average over that step, slightly steeper than the value at the point itself. Shrink h and 4.1 walks towards 4.
