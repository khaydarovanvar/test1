Teacher script

The spoken script for the “Why do we need math?” lesson — one part per page. Read it from your phone while the projector shows the deck. Green lines tell you what to press or wait for. Don’t read it word for word — it is a support, not a cage.

About timing. The full material runs 60–70 minutes. For a 45-minute lesson: open only 3–4 cards in section 03, pick 2 cases in section 12, and save section 13 for the next lesson — it makes a perfect warm-up recap. Never rush section 09.
00

Before the bell

−5 min
💻 Open your netlify.app address → F (fullscreen) → P → Live voting: Turn on. Leave the QR join screen up. Pick the language with L.

— Yes, you heard me correctly: phones out. Scan the code on the screen. This may be the only lesson all year where I ask you to keep your phone in your hand. Enjoy it.

[wait until the “votes in” counter is close to the class size → press J to return to the lesson]

No Wi-Fi? Nothing changes: students raise hands and you tap the answer buttons yourself.

01

The hook & the first vote

≈4 min
📺 The title page. Keep the vote block in view.

— Every year, in the first week, somebody asks me these three questions. [gesture at the bubbles] Why do I need trigonometry? Why derivatives? Why integrals? Usually it gets asked around October, in a quieter voice. This year we are not waiting for October. We do it today, in the open, lesson one.

— We start by voting. Be honest — properly honest. Nobody can see your answer, only the totals, and I am not grading this. How useful is math for your future?

[wait; when “votes in” stops climbing:] → press “Show the class”

— Interesting picture. Remember it: at the end of the lesson we open this same vote again, and we see who would move their answer. Who is right — I am not saying yet.

02

Body vs brain

≈5 min
📺 The drag-and-drop cards. You can invite one or two students up to sort them.

— Strange question. Who here does sport? [hands] Now tell me: name one moment in real life where you must lift a heavy metal bar above your head. … There isn’t one. Nobody’s grandmother asks them to bench-press the furniture.

— And yet nobody walks into a gym and says “when will I ever use this barbell?” Because everyone understands: the barbell is not the point. What it builds is the point. Let’s sort these cards: which results come from training the body, which from training the mind?

[when the cards are placed] → press “Check the class”

— Look at the two traps: concentration and discipline. They fit both columns. The whole secret of this course is in those two words. Math is the gym. The integral is a barbell. You will put it down after the exam. What you built lifting it — stays.

03

Where it lives

≈4 min
📺 The 11 tiles. Don’t open them all — take 3–4 the class calls out. GPS and AI land hardest.

— “Training” sounds abstract. Different game. Everything on this screen is something you used this morning or will use tonight. Call one out — I’ll open it.

[tap the tiles they call; read the explanation aloud]

— Now the uncomfortable part: not one of these could exist without the exact topics on this year’s program. The math has not disappeared from the world. It moved inside the machines — and somebody has to speak its language.

04

The trigonometry challenge

≈5 min
📺 The building problem. The triangle animates when an answer is tapped.

— A real one. You are 30 metres from a building. You look at the top — the angle is 45 degrees. No ladder, no drone, the tape measure is too short. How tall is the building? A, B or C — hands up.

[count hands for each option, then tap the correct answer]

— 30 metres. Because tan 45° is exactly one — the height equals the distance. You just measured a building with a protractor. That is what trigonometry really is: not triangles, relationships. The thing you can measure tells you the thing you cannot. Builders, pilots, game designers, GPS — the same trick, every single day.

05

The derivative — mathematics of change

≈5 min
📺 The car and the question. The slider appears after the answer — drag it SLOWLY.

— The car is moving. I tell you: it has gone 200 metres. Is that enough to know? [vote] No. Because 200 metres crawling and 200 metres about to crash are completely different situations. What you actually want to know is: how fast is it changing, right now?

[drag the time slider slowly; point at the three panels]

— Watch. Position. Speed — how fast position changes. Acceleration — how fast speed changes. That single operation — “how fast is this changing right now?” — has a name: the derivative. That’s it. That is the entire scary word. Your speedometer computes one every second.

06

The integral — pieces to the whole

≈5 min
📺 The area under the curve. After the answer: press 10 → 50 → 100 → 1000 in order and watch the ERROR tile.

— Now the opposite question. Here is a curved shape. How do we find its area — guess, ruler, or…? [they pick C] Rectangles. Now watch the error. Ten — error 0.1. Fifty — 0.004. A thousand — almost nothing. When the strips get infinitely thin, the answer stops moving. That is the integral: adding infinitely many, infinitely small pieces.

— The most beautiful part: derivative and integral are one idea, run in opposite directions. Change, and accumulation. Speed becomes distance. That pair runs every engine, rocket, animation and AI model on earth.

07

Money: cash or credit

≈5 min
📺 The loan calculator. Move the sliders in front of the class. Then the phone vote.

— Now the part nobody teaches you until it costs you money. You are 25. The car is thirty thousand dollars. Cash, or credit? Let’s look with open eyes. [sliders] 637 a month. Doesn’t sound bad, does it? Total: 38,245. The same car. Eight thousand dollars extra — a quarter of the car — just for the arrangement of the payment.

[phone vote → “Show the class”]

— There is no single right answer here — sometimes you need the car now. But there is a right way to decide: with the total in front of your eyes. That is the difference.

08

The “only $650 a month” trap

≈4 min
📺 The advert banner. Read it out loud, in your best advertising voice.

— [advertising voice] “Only 650 a month! Nothing up front! Drive it today!” … Sounds cheap? Vote.

[vote → press “Now do the arithmetic the advert avoided”]

— The advert shows you one number. You do the operation it is hiding: 650 times 60 — 39,000. Never judge a loan by its monthly payment. Ten seconds of multiplication — the most profitable arithmetic of your life.

09

Riba & structure

≈4 min
📺 The Islamic finance section. Do not rush it. Tone — respectful and calm.

— Now a question some of you are already thinking about. In Islamic finance, riba — interest — is prohibited, so a conventional loan is generally regarded as impermissible. I am a mathematics teacher, not a scholar, and this lesson is not a fatwa. But I want you to see one thing: whatever contract you are offered — conventional, murabaha, ijarah — mathematics is how you read it.

— “Don’t worry, it’s only 500 a month.” What does a mathematically educated person ask? Mark them.

[let them pick → press “Check answers”]

— How much in total. How it was calculated. For how long. Those three questions protect you in any system. And for a real decision — ask a qualified scholar and do the arithmetic. Neither replaces the other.

10

The doubling dollar

≈5 min
📺 The two big cards. After the vote: press “Run 30 days” — then stay quiet and let the chart speak.

— Choice time, and you cannot change your mind. A thousand dollars today — or one dollar that doubles every day for a month. Vote on your phones. Think carefully.

[vote → “Everyone voted? Reveal the 30 days” → ▶ play]

— Day ten: 512 dollars. Still losing. Day twenty: half a million. Day thirty: 536 million dollars. Your intuition thinks in straight lines. The world’s money, debts, viruses and followers grow exponentially. That is not an insult to your brain. It is the reason mathematics exists: to see what intuition cannot.

11

The decision tool

≈3 min
📺 The $10,000 and the six information cards.

— Someone hands you ten thousand dollars. Spend, save, invest? Which is right — I won’t say. Different question: what would you need to know before deciding? Mark everything you would want.

[let them pick → press “Check the class”]

— All six. And notice what happened: mathematics did not hand you the decision. It never does. It turned a feeling into questions with comparable answers. That is the difference between a decision and a guess.

12

The math detective

≈5 min
📺 The +50% / −50% case and 5 quick cases. Short on time — pick two (cases 2 and 5 are the best).

— You get a promotion: detective. Percentages are where ordinary people get robbed — politely, legally, daily. First case together: the price goes up 50 percent, then down 50 percent. Are we back? No — you are 125 dollars short. A percentage is never taken from the same base twice. Now on your own: the score counts.

[work the cases one by one; read the explanation aloud after each answer]
13

Rapid fire

≈7 min
📺 7 questions, 10 seconds each. The class shouts, you tap. In a 45-minute lesson: save this for next time.

— The final game. Seven questions, ten seconds each, all from what we touched today. Simple rule: the class shouts, I tap. I press whatever most of you shout — so you have ten seconds to convince each other. Ready?

[▶ Start; at the end read the board: where did we fall, and why]
14

The big idea

≈2 min
📺 The dark screen. Press ▶ and be QUIET. The lines appear by themselves — read them slowly, out loud. Let this be the quietest minute of the lesson.

— Quiet for a minute. Just read with me. [as the lines land] You may forget a formula. You may never calculate an integral after school. You may never use the sine rule in your job. [pause] But you will always need to think.

15

The last question & the close

≈3 min
📺 The final quiz → the closing screen → “← Back to the first vote”.

— Last question of the day. What is mathematics, really? [they pick D] Numbers are the alphabet. Formulas are the sentences. The exam is just someone checking you can read. Mathematics itself is a language — the one humanity invented to describe patterns, relationships and change.

[“← Back to the first vote” → the first bars again]

— This is how you voted at the start of the lesson. Now tell me: who would move their answer? Which way — and why? [hear two or three students] Homework: nothing to solve. Just one job: tomorrow, catch one number in the wild — a discount, a loan advert, a statistic — and bring it here. We will interrogate it together. Lesson over. Thank you.
