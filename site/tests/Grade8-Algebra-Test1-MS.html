B

Section B · Two marks each

Method marks are available even when the final answer is wrong.

Medium14 marks
  1. 11

    Simplify: (3a3b2)2 [2]

    Answer9a6b4

    WorkingSquare every factor: 32 = 9, (a3)2 = a6, (b2)2 = b4.

    A common slip is 3a6b4 — the 3 must be squared as well.

  2. 12

    Expand and simplify: (x + 5)(x − 2) − x2 [2]

    Answer3x − 10

    Working(x + 5)(x − 2) = x2 + 3x − 10; subtracting x2 leaves 3x − 10.
  3. 13

    Factorise: 9x2 − 16y2 [2]

    Answer(3x − 4y)(3x + 4y)

    WorkingA difference of two squares, since 9x2 = (3x)2 and 16y2 = (4y)2.
  4. 14

    Cancel: ⁠3x + 12x2 − 16⁠ [2]

    Answer⁠3x − 4⁠,  x ≠ 4, x ≠ −4

    WorkingFactorise first: ⁠3(x + 4)(x − 4)(x + 4)⁠. The factor (x + 4) cancels.
  5. 15

    State the permissible values of ⁠x + 2x2 − 25⁠ [2]

    Answerx ≠ 5, x ≠ −5

    Workingx2 − 25 = (x − 5)(x + 5), which is zero at x = 5 and at x = −5.
  6. 16

    Find the value of ⁠a2 − b2a + b⁠ when a = 9, b = 4 [2]

    Answer5

    WorkingCancel before substituting: ⁠(a − b)(a + b)a + b⁠ = a − b = 9 − 4 = 5.

    Substituting first gives ⁠6513⁠, which is also 5 — but cancelling first is the skill being tested.

  7. 17

    For which value of x is ⁠x − 7x + 1⁠ equal to zero? [2]

    Answerx = 7

    WorkingA fraction is zero when its numerator is zero and its denominator is not: x − 7 = 0 gives x = 7, and x = 7 is permissible because x ≠ −1.

    An answer of x = 7 or x = −1 shows the two conditions have been confused.

C

Section C · Four marks each

Factorise before you cancel, and state the values the variable may not take.

Hard16 marks
  1. 18

    (a) Factorise completely: x3 − 9x  [2]
    (b) Hence cancel ⁠x3 − 9xx2 + 3x⁠ and state the permissible values.  [2] [4]

    Answer(a) x(x − 3)(x + 3)    (b) x − 3,  x ≠ 0, x ≠ −3

    Working(a) Take out x first, giving x(x2 − 9), then the difference of two squares gives x(x − 3)(x + 3).
    (b) The denominator factorises as x(x + 3), so ⁠x(x − 3)(x + 3)x(x + 3)⁠ = x − 3. That denominator is zero at x = 0 and at x = −3, so both values are excluded.

    “Completely” means going on until nothing factorises further; stopping at x(x2 − 9) loses a mark.

  2. 19

    Cancel ⁠x2 − 8x + 16x2 − 16⁠, state the permissible values, then find the value of your answer when x = 9. [4]

    Answer⁠x − 4x + 4⁠,  x ≠ 4, x ≠ −4;   at x = 9 it is ⁠513⁠

    WorkingThe numerator is a perfect square: x2 − 8x + 16 = (x − 4)2. The denominator is (x − 4)(x + 4). One factor of (x − 4) cancels and leaves ⁠x − 4x + 4⁠.
    At x = 9: ⁠9 − 49 + 4⁠ = ⁠513⁠.
  3. 20

    (a) Cancel: ⁠2x2 − 2y23y − 3x⁠  [3]
    (b) Explain in one sentence why ⁠x + 55⁠ cannot be cancelled.  [1] [4]

    Answer(a) −⁠2(x + y)3⁠,  x ≠ y    (b) because the 5 is a term, not a factor

    Working(a) 2x2 − 2y2 = 2(x − y)(x + y) and 3y − 3x = −3(x − y). The swap y − x = −(x − y) is what makes the two factors match; cancelling (x − y) leaves −⁠2(x + y)3⁠.
    (b) In x + 5 the 5 is a term, not a factor, and only a factor common to the whole numerator and the whole denominator may be cancelled.

    Part (b) separates students who follow the rule from students who know why it holds.

  4. 21

    Cancel ⁠2x2 + 5x − 3x2 − 9⁠, stating the permissible values. [4]

    Answer⁠2x − 1x − 3⁠,  x ≠ 3, x ≠ −3

    WorkingThe numerator factorises as (2x − 1)(x + 3) — check by expanding: 2x2 + 6x − x − 3 = 2x2 + 5x − 3. The denominator is (x − 3)(x + 3), so the factor (x + 3) cancels.
