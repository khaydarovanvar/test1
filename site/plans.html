How they are built

The same method in every year

Each plan starts from the official KTP workbook and keeps it exactly: the same topics, the same order, the same hours per quarter. A generator script asserts both, so a plan cannot drift out of compliance by accident, and a second script checks the generated plan back against the source workbook lesson by lesson.

Cambridge coverage is then added in two ways. Every national lesson carries the Cambridge sub-unit it corresponds to; where the national topic goes further, the note reads Extension beyond. And the lessons the national plan leaves flexible — the chapter-exercise lessons, the “practical and cross-curricular problems”, the revision blocks — are filled with the Cambridge content the national programme does not reach at all. Those are flagged CAM.

Grade 6 is the one exception, because it is taught six hours a week against a five-hour national plan. Its 34 spare lessons carry Stage 7 content outright — above all directed numbers, which the national programme does not reach until Grade 7.
